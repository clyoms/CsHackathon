Frame v3: eight layouts to pick from

Grey boxes only. Pick a structure first, then we style it. Each board shows the home page and one inside page (the employee page). Colour here only marks the role, so you can see how each layout tells you where you are.

1. Role doors

Split screen

The home page is three tall full-height panels, one per role. Hover or tap widens a panel and shows its promise and live number. Inside, a thin bar in the role colour runs across the top.

Good
Strong first moment for judges. Role choice is the whole screen.
Risk
Skills and Literacy are hidden until you pick a role.
Skill match
Exaggerated minimalism

2. Persistent role sidebar

App shell

A left sidebar on every page, grouped by role. The active role's block is filled with its colour. Home is a welcome with three large cards. Feels like a real product people log into.

Good
Clearest "where am I". Easiest to build and keep consistent.
Risk
Safe. Can look like any admin tool unless styling is bold.
Skill match
Data-dense dashboard

3. Bento command centre

Tile grid

Home is a grid of tiles in mixed sizes. Each role gets a big tile with its live number. Smaller tiles show the next deadline, a skill of the week and literacy progress. Inside pages use the same tiles.

Good
Shows lots at once without feeling busy. Easy to rearrange.
Risk
Bento grids are very common in 2026 SaaS pages.
Skill match
Bento box grid

4. One question first

Search-led

Home is one giant box: "What do you need today?" Type, and Athena routes you to the right role page. Three role chips sit under the box for people who would rather click. Inside pages keep the box at the top.

Good
Shows off Athena from second one. Simplest for non-technical staff.
Risk
Partner and AI Lead need data views, which a box does not show.
Skill match
Exaggerated minimalism

5. Deadline calendar

Timeline

A wide calendar strip across the top of every page, showing the next six weeks of Irish tax deadlines. Below it, three role lanes. Whatever you click, the calendar stays, so the firm's real pressure is always in view.

Good
Only an accounting firm would have this. Ties every number to a date.
Risk
Calendar strip needs care on a phone (scrolls inside itself).
Skill match
Data-dense dashboard

6. Bold blocks

Neubrutalist

Thick black outlines, hard offset shadows, flat colour blocks, oversized type. Role cards look like stacked index cards. It is loud, playful and nothing like a bank portal.

Good
Stands out most. Judges will remember it.
Risk
May feel too playful for partners at a tax firm.
Skill match
Neubrutalism

7. Journey map

Guided path

Home shows one path from left to right: Learn (AI literacy), Try (employee prompt), Prove (skills library), Track (AI Lead), Decide (partner). Each role starts at its own stop. The literacy course is the front door, not an extra.

Good
Tells the whole pitch story. Puts the new 4D course up front.
Risk
Repeat visitors may find the path slow.
Skill match
Progress indicators, landing pattern

8. Role colour worlds

Themed pages

The top nav is shared, but each role's page takes over the whole background with its own colour and its own big number in the corner. Changing role feels like walking into a different room. Home is a large three-row stack you scroll through.

Good
Impossible to forget which role you are in. Big, confident colour.
Risk
Three strong colours must pass contrast checks in light and dark.
Skill match
Exaggerated minimalism plus vibrant blocks

My pick, if you want one

  1. Option 2 (sidebar) as the structure, because it answers "where am I" on every page and Skills and Literacy are always one click away.
  2. Option 8's colour worlds on top, so each role page fills with its colour and it stops looking plain.
  3. Option 5's calendar strip on the partner and employee pages, because it is the one detail only a tax firm would have.
